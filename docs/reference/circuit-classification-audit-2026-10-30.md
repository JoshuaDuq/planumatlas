# Classification audit of the Circuits catalogue

Review date: 30 October 2026. Catalogue revision inspected: `2026-10-30`.

## Finding and recommendation

All 39 entries were inspected for their authored route, landmarks, scope and cited evidence roles. Under the requested strict anatomical criterion, recommend moving 16 current entries to clinical/functional landmark tours, and moving or redesigning another 15 network/function tours. Five entries can remain as qualified pathway or loop-branch tours. Three require a more specific anatomical representation before they should be presented as circuit demonstrations.

This is a recommendation about the **current tours**, not a declaration that these functions lack neural circuits or that their cited research is invalid. The recommendations were applied on 31 October as recorded below.

## Applied on 31 October 2026

The active catalogue now contains five tours, 24 landmarks and 22 references: visual pathway, hippocampal–diencephalic pathway, basal ganglia motor loops, cerebellar output and crossing, and corticospinal descent and crossing. The memory tour’s title was narrowed in English and French; its anatomy and scientific notes are preserved.

The 16 clinical/functional landmark tours and 15 network/function tours were withdrawn from the Circuits interface. Auditory, somatosensory and reward/valuation were also withheld pending anatomical rework. All 34 withdrawn records, including their bilingual descriptions, atlas mappings and citations, are preserved in [`data/archived-circuit-tours.yaml`](https://github.com/JoshuaDuq/neuroatlas/blob/main/data/archived-circuit-tours.yaml); this file is not loaded by the viewer. Clinical deficit profiles and functional maps remain available in their existing sections. No separate Networks or Clinical Tours interface was added.

Archived references remain included in identity checks and reference metadata retrieval. Archive mappings remain checked against the published anatomy. Links to withdrawn circuit IDs use the existing unsupported-link handling: the circuit and step are rejected while other valid scene fields remain. Existing links to the five retained IDs continue to resolve.

Later on 31 October, two retained tours were made more specific. The memory tour ends on NextBrain’s anteroventral thalamic nucleus instead of the whole thalamus, following the mammillothalamic projection described by [Bubb et al. (2017)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5608081/). The cerebellar tour adds the right red nucleus as a waypoint after the crossing, citing the human histology of [Gallay et al. (2008)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2494572/). The catalogue now has 25 landmarks and 23 references.

The tables below document the 39-entry catalogue before the cleanup. They are a historical classification record, not the current list of selectable circuits. These changes do not constitute independent clinical validation.

## Criterion and limits

A circuit can branch, recur and interact with other circuits. It need not be a closed loop or a simple serial chain. For this category, the tour should teach a specified set of anatomical connections, supported by appropriate connectional evidence, and distinguish the actual relays from coarse reference parcels. Lesion–behaviour associations or coactivation alone do not establish a connection between displayed regions. A textbook pathway does not have to be reconstructed by tractography in this individual to be a valid teaching subject.

This was a catalogue-wide classification audit with targeted rechecking of cited studies, not a complete full-text review of every source or a new validation of every anatomical edge. Categories below are editorial recommendations, not evidence grades. Human imaging, human lesions and animal tracing must retain their distinct evidence roles.

## Targeted source checks informing the recommendations

- [Rapp et al. (2016)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26685156/), [Roswandowitz et al. (2018)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29228111/) and [Chen et al. (2016)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27182489/): lesion mapping supplies relevant regional associations, not the connections implied by treating their selected parcels as complete circuits.
- [Dosenbach et al. (2007)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17576922/): the cognitive-control background distinguishes two networks; combining their sampled nodes is not a demonstration of one executive circuit.
- [Bartlett (2013)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3707394/): the auditory thalamic subdivisions have different connections; a dorsal MGB highlight cannot substitute for the ventral relay used in the principal ascending pathway to A1.
- [Aggleton et al. (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10804970/): the memory pathway branch should not be promoted to a uniquely necessary serial Papez model.
- [Kelly & Strick (2003)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6740694/): tracing provides connectional evidence for cerebello-cortical loops in nonhuman primates. It supports teaching the loops with a stated species basis; it does not turn a selection of human posterior lobules into a complete displayed loop.

The existing catalogue already states many of these limits. The main problem is the umbrella category and what the tours imply when described collectively as established, validated circuits. Existing clinical references and useful teaching material can be preserved outside that category.
