## Inclusion decisions

Selection required human patient evidence in peer-reviewed primary research, a distinguishable impairment, and complementary evidence supporting or qualifying inclusion. These profiles separate weakness on deliberate facial effort from weakness during spontaneous emotion; both differ from learned oral-gesture planning and emotion recognition. Cough flow on command is distinguished from reflex cough, swallowing and aspiration. Urinary retention and urine leakage are separate symptoms that can coexist, with several neurological, functional and non-neurological mechanisms.

This is a targeted content review, not a systematic review or independent specialist assessment. Case reports support possible motor dissociations and selected anatomical associations, not incidence or a universally sufficient lesion. Clinical referral cohorts do not establish population prevalence. Counts are not pooled and publication samples are not presumed independent. Hopf's 1992 paper contributes different cases to both facial profiles; it does not supply two independent publications. Animal physiology and healthy activation studies are not substituted for observed patient injury.

## Source identity and extraction scope

The 11 included PMIDs, titles, author lists, issue years and DOIs were checked with the existing PubMed/Crossref reference-checking code. No linked correction, retraction or expression of concern was returned for these selected works. Pizzi's issue year is 2014 despite its 2013 online record; Umemura's issue year is 2016. Only the 11 selected works were merged into the reference cache, preserving all earlier values. The cache's `fetched` date describes incremental verification, not complete retrieval of old sources.

Four references use full-text scope. This means consultation beyond the abstract, with the extraction areas below. Source image pixels, lesion masks, raw urodynamic traces and original clinical recordings were not independently reanalyzed.

For Hopf (2000), the complete short clinical report and figure caption were read. The 63-year-old patient had a right superior cerebellar artery territory infarct involving right dorsolateral pontine tegmentum and cerebellum. The figure caption explicitly identifies left emotional facial paresis with symmetric showing of the teeth on command. Other crossed sensory, sympathetic and cerebellar signs make this a multi-structure lesion, not selective destruction of an emotional facial pathway. The whole pontine landmark is therefore confined to the documented component and does not reconstruct the entire infarct.

For Prabhakar, selection, imaging and statistical methods, Results and Discussion/limitations were read. Thirty-one isolated lateral medullary infarcts were identified; eight had recorded micturition dysfunction, with two local prostatic outlet causes excluded from neurological attribution. All six remaining retention cases had urological evaluation, but only one had urodynamics. That patient's detrusor underactivity is not generalized as directly measured in all six. Symptoms began within five days, and four retention lesions were right-sided and two left-sided. Images were flipped to the left for analysis; only voxels affected in at least five patients were tested, with 5% false-discovery-rate correction. The gross medullary landmarks do not reproduce that small tegmental result or directly identify a descending tract. Recovery information was limited. The article's 31 identified cases and two urological exclusions are reported without inventing a more precise analyzed-group denominator or a broadly applicable prevalence.

For Tish, the complete main case description, Introduction and Discussion were read. The 70-year-old man had expanding right pontine tegmental hemorrhage, midbrain extension and edema partly crossing the midline. He reported bladder fullness but had residuals above one litre after two failed voiding trials and recovered sufficient voluntary voiding at about one month. This observed course is not a prognosis. The source explicitly leaves open the possibility that left-sided injury also disrupts voiding and notes that catheterization in unconscious patients may complicate detection. The retained landmark does not make this a selectively injured micturition nucleus or a purely right-sided circuit.

For Gupta, the inclusion/exclusion criteria, urodynamic procedures, broad imaging classification, Results/table and Discussion/limitations were read. The 40 patients were referred for urinary symptoms, with both arterial and venous stroke and ages 18–80. Premorbid urinary/prostatic complaints, obstruction, altered sensorium and global aphasia were excluded. Testing occurred a mean of 28 days after stroke, ranging from eight to 53 days. Thirty-six had detrusor overactivity, three underactivity and one a normal study. Broad-site and side correlations were not significant. The small selected cohort, categorical anatomical descriptions and absence of systematic follow-up do not justify a regional prediction rule. Uncontrolled treatment responses are not used as efficacy evidence.

The remaining seven references retain abstract scope. Selected indexed main-text passages were also consulted for Ward's cough-analysis exclusions and measurement limitations; this does not upgrade the study to full-text extraction. Its 18 enrolled patients are not all assumed to contribute to every cough measurement: two could not perform voluntary cough and were excluded from its intensity analysis. Preservation on a stimulated expiratory-muscle test does not prove that every respiratory muscle or component of cough is intact. Pizzi's repeat urodynamics in 63 of 106 participants is not represented as complete follow-up. The catalogue now contains 71 full-text and 174 abstract-based reference records. Earlier addition reviews and the [full-text review log](full-text-review.md) retain their historical scopes.

## Screened anatomical evidence not imported

[Lee et al. (2020), Effect of Brain Lesions on Voluntary Cough in Patients with Supratentorial Stroke](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32927900/), was examined in full-text main methods, Results and Discussion. It is a peer-reviewed primary study, but its 39 patients were selected for subjective cough weakness, images were flipped to one side and the described voxelwise threshold is P < .05 without a stated voxelwise multiple-comparison correction. The cohort also had substantial dysphagia overlap. A non-significant lesion-volume correlation is not sufficient to establish absence of confounding. These methods do not justify importing the paper's broad temporal, parietal or white-matter regions as a precise cough map here. This is an extraction and mapping decision, not a claim that the article is retracted or that its clinical observations are invalid. The profile's inclusion rests on the controlled physiological study and the larger clinical cohort above. This screened article is not among the 11 added catalogue references or cached records.
