Review date and catalogue revision: `2026-11-01`.

## Inclusion decisions

Inclusion required human patient evidence in peer-reviewed primary research, a deficit distinct from the existing profiles, and complementary publications supporting or qualifying the finding. A single case or healthy activation alone was insufficient. Multiple papers are not assumed to be independent replications. This targeted educational review is not a systematic review or independent specialist assessment.

| Impaired visual verticality perception | [Brandt et al. (1994)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8154866/) studied 71 unilateral infarcts; 23 of 52 middle cerebral artery infarcts had pathological visual vertical tilts. [Baier et al. (2012)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22357719/) mapped 54 cortical strokes. A separate [79-patient brainstem study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23100408/) distinguished ipsiversive from contraversive tilt associations. | Posterior-insular parcels are coarse landmarks, not reconstructed overlap. The whole-gyrus inferior frontal label does not resolve a subdivision, and the named brainstem nuclei and pathways are not replaced by whole pons or midbrain parcels. Crucially, [ten isolated insular strokes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23868267/) produced no visual vertical tilt or other measured otolith deficit: isolated insular injury is not established as sufficient. Visual verticality is distinct from bodily posture and lateropulsion. |
| Source-memory impairment | [Janowsky et al. (1989)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2797412/) tested facts and their learning context. [Swick et al. (2006)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16828722/) compared nine unilateral lateral frontal patients with nine older and nine younger controls using words and voices. [Chapados & Petrides (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25567650/) found different effects of right ventrolateral and left dorsomedial lesions depending on context stability. | Whole middle frontal gyri mark Swick's posterior lateral BA 9/46 lesion centres, with seven left and two right cases; they are not precise fields or ERP generators. Item recognition was also impaired. Disproportionate source impairment versus older controls was not significant for the full patient group, but was present in a four-patient item-matched subset. Chapados's abstract does not justify specific gyral borders, so that finding remains uncoloured. [Stamenova et al. (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28499557/) found preserved source memory across their focal-lesion groups, qualifying any universal frontal-deficit claim. |
| Motor impersistence | [Kertesz et al. (1985)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3990966/) compared age- and lesion-size-matched acute stroke groups and controls; [De Renzi et al. (1986)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3951712/) tested cooperative unilateral-stroke admissions over ten months with 50 controls. Both found a right-hemisphere predominance. | Failure to sustain actions is separated from failure to initiate them. Broad central/frontal locations remain uncoloured. A [right medial frontal/callosal case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17353476/) showed dominant right-limb impersistence influenced by neglected space, qualifying a simple contralateral-limb rule; it does not resolve a callosal subdivision. The case is not the sole inclusion evidence. |
| Utilization behaviour | [Lhermitte et al. (1986)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3707084/) describe imitation and utilization behaviour with frontal lesions. [Besnard et al. (2010)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20211049/) compare elicitation procedures in 25 frontal-lesion patients, ten posterior-lesion patients and 35 controls. [Shallice et al. (1989)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2597999/) describe incidental object use in a bifrontal case. | The early 96% figure pools imitation and utilization, so it is not utilization-only prevalence. Task content, presentation and instructions affect elicitation. Inferior anterior and medial bifrontal descriptions are too broad to isolate orbitofrontal, rectus or cingulate parcels; no parcel is selected. |
| Lesion-associated freezing of gait | [Fasano et al. (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28009063/) analyze 14 published cases with heterogeneous lesions and a 98-person healthy connectome. Thirteen lesion locations connect to dorsal medial cerebellar regions. [Fève et al. (1993)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8341296/) describe four post-hypoxic patients with MRI pallidal lesions and axial signs including freezing. | This profile concerns episodic stepping failure, distinct from weakness or inability to stand. Network connectivity does not establish direct injury in every connected structure. Cerebellar connectivity distinguished freezing from the two movement-disorder comparison groups; thalamic and subcallosal connectivity did not. Whole-brain anoxia was excluded from the network sample, so the hypoxic series is complementary clinical evidence rather than a direct replication. Its abstract does not specify laterality, precluding bilateral pallidal paint. All findings remain uncoloured; no treatment target or generalization to all Parkinson-related freezing is validated. |
| Post-traumatic anosmia and hyposmia | [Yousem et al. (1999)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10228615/) studied 36 patients with psychophysical testing and MRI. [Rombaux et al. (2006)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16735894/) tested orthonasal and retronasal olfaction in 25 patients. [Doty et al. (1997)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9311357/) report 268 referred patients, and [Kim et al. (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25177434/) describe MRI and odor identification in 19 adults. | The profile concerns complete or partial smell loss and does not equate identification with detection or all head trauma with cortical injury. Referral samples do not estimate population prevalence, and related-centre samples may overlap. Five of Kim's patients had no visible MRI abnormality; its early and delayed groups are different patients. Bulbs and tracts are not replaced by similarly named sulcal or nuclear parcels. Broad orbitofrontal categories remain uncoloured. |
| Micrographia after acquired brain injury | [Derkinderen et al. (2002)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12210889/) describe four left lenticular infarct cases, two with writer's cramp. [Denes et al. (2005)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16006345/) describe a traumatic case; [Nelson et al. (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25478259/) provide an MRI-defined pallidal case. | The whole left pallidum marks Nelson's acute/subacute infarct only, not all lenticular lesions or a proven isolated cause. The case also had an older right pallidal infarct, possible unresolved thalamic involvement and broader cognitive/language deficits, with no formal follow-up. Small case-based evidence does not establish prevalence or a universal hemisphere rule. Handwriting size is distinguished from linguistic spelling and from a diagnosis of Parkinson's disease. |
| Hypersomnia after thalamic stroke | [Bassetti et al. (1996)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8619525/) examined 12 selected hypersomnolent patients. [Hermann et al. (2008)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18048862/) studied 46 consecutive paramedian strokes, with polysomnography in 31 and one-year functional follow-up in 31. [Hansen et al. (2020)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33287737/) describe persistent hypersomnia after posterior right thalamic injury. | Whole thalami are coarse landmarks for Hermann's 16 left, 16 right and 14 bilateral cases, not exact paramedian nuclei. Sleep need improved substantially; no permanent-deficit rule is implied. Hansen's main case also reports cerebellar infarcts despite its discussion claiming no other structural changes, so no isolated pulvinar cause or pulvinar-only parcel is asserted. The case's retelling of earlier sample sizes is not substituted for Hermann's primary 46-patient count. |
| Saccadic dysmetria | [Bötzel et al. (1993)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8461969/) compared 24 patients with cerebellar/connection lesions and 17 controls. [Büttner et al. (1994)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8021670/) describe two bilateral midline-lesion cases with fastigial involvement, overshooting saccades and preserved smooth pursuit. | Whole fastigial nuclei are reference landmarks for the two cases, shown in learning and histological views, without isolating a functional subdivision or excluding neighbouring injury. Small normal undershoot is not itself diagnostic. The larger study found diverse primary/corrective saccade patterns, including two patients with normal saccades in every tested condition. |
| Impaired smooth pursuit | [Baier et al. (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19567703/) compared 11 cerebellar-stroke patients with deficient pursuit gain and 17 with intact gain. [Lekwuwa & Barnes (1996)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8800943/) studied 72 focal cerebral lesions, including 20 patients with normal pursuit at all tested frequencies. | Vermis IX (uvula) and VIII (pyramis) are landmarks for Baier's uvular and partial pyramidal overlap. Whole VIII exceeds the partial overlap; hemispheric lobule IX is not substituted for the vermal uvula. Distributed cerebral associations remain uncoloured because the abstract does not resolve defensible parcel boundaries. Büttner's preserved-pursuit cases qualify any claim that all cerebellar ocular motor injury impairs pursuit. |

## Source verification and extraction scope

All 30 included PMIDs were checked across the two passes using the existing PubMed/Crossref reference-checking code for title, authors, journal-issue year, DOI and linked notices. No linked correction, retraction or expression of concern was returned. Only these included works were merged into the reference cache. Its `fetched` date records the incremental update; it does not imply a fresh retrieval of older records.

Five added references use full-text scope. For Swick, the main Introduction, Results, Discussion and Experimental procedures, Tables 1–3 and figure captions were read. These establish lesion laterality and BA 9/46 centres, the task, item and source results, the non-significant full-group source-by-item interaction against older controls, and the four-patient subset. The young participants' near-ceiling performance, the predominantly left-sided sample, variable lesion extensions and some patients' language difficulties limit inference. No ERP scalp effect is mapped as a discrete cortical generator.

For Fasano, the main Methods, Results, Discussion and Limitations were read. Inclusion/exclusion criteria, manual tracing of published lesion images, the healthy connectome, the seven-case vascular sensitivity analysis and comparison-condition specificity were checked. The authors did not examine these patients prospectively, and comparison with asterixis and hemichorea does not establish specificity against every gait disorder. Mechanistic and treatment suggestions remain hypotheses. No supplemental case lists, imaging pixels or videos were independently reinterpreted, and no lesion masks or numerical analyses were reproduced.

For Kim, the main Methods, Results and Discussion and figure captions were read. All 19 patients had patent olfactory clefts, the outcome was an identification test rather than an isolated detection threshold, and the groups were cross-sectional. The five normal MRI studies and possible referral bias qualify a universal structural-lesion claim. Patient-table images were not independently transcribed. For Nelson, the full case, Discussion and captions establish the acute left and older right pallidal infarcts, predominantly right-hand micrographia, broader deficits, uncertain thalamic exclusion and lack of formal follow-up.

For Hansen, the main case, Discussion and captions were read. The main case reports infarcts in both cerebellar hemispheres as well as right posterior thalamus; supplemental figure descriptions also mention cerebellar infarction. This conflicts with the discussion's statement that there were no other structural changes. The finding preserves that confound and does not repeat the abstract's implied isolated-thalamic localization. Treatment descriptions from this single case are not converted into efficacy claims. Supplemental image pixels were not independently reviewed. Bassetti, Hermann and Yousem have PubMed adolescent indexing; their consulted abstracts do not establish exclusively adult age ranges, and no exclusively adult population is claimed for those cohorts.

Across both passes, the other 25 added references retain abstract scope. Available indexed text and captions do not upgrade an incomplete extraction to full-text review. Counts absent from an abstract are explicitly left unspecified. The catalogue now contains 55 full-text and 132 abstract records. The earlier [full-text review log](full-text-review.md) retains its historical scope.

## Excluded candidate evidence

An additional [2020 electrophysiological smell-testing report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32982956/) was reviewed in full but not added to the catalogue or citation cache. Its perception-response counts differ between the Results prose and Table 2, and its central/peripheral localization claims do not come from focal lesion mapping. Its statements about identifying malingering are not adopted. The smell-loss profile instead uses the four clinical and imaging studies listed above; the excluded paper is not counted among the 30 included references.
