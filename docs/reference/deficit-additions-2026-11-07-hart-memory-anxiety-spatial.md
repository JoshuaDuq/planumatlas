# Hart deficit additions: memory, anxiety and spatial delusions

Review date and catalogue revision: `2026-11-07`.

This third Hart pass follows the [further Hart additions](deficit-additions-2026-11-07-hart-further.md). It adds two bilingual profiles, ten findings and 14 references. The catalogue now contains 131 profiles, 218 findings and 339 references. The baseline for this pass contains 129 profiles, 208 findings and 325 references. Five of the new findings extend existing profiles: three for working memory, one for amnesia and one for semantic memory. The working-memory and semantic-memory introductions were widened to cover them, and semantic memory gained three search terms. Hart's textbook supplies research leads, while the findings and coloured landmarks below depend on the cited patient studies.

## Anxiety symptoms after brain injury

[Knutson et al. (2013)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3644343/) mapped lesions in 182 Vietnam veterans with chronic, mainly penetrating brain injuries. Observer-rated anxiety (Neurobehavioral Rating Scale) was associated with a predominantly left temporal-limbic cluster, including the hippocampus, and with a small right superior/middle temporal cluster after false-discovery-rate correction. Informant ratings (Neuropsychiatric Inventory) overlapped the left-sided result. Self-reported anxiety showed no lesion association. The injured veterans did not differ from 51 uninjured combat veterans overall, and the affected-region group differed from them only at trend level (p = .059). That group also had greater brain-volume loss than veterans with lesions elsewhere. [Ku et al. (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23588227/) independently associated right superior and middle temporal injury with anxiety one month after middle cerebral artery stroke in 26 patients. Ku remains abstract-scope.

The temporal-limbic finding colours the left hippocampus and both middle temporal gyri as parts of the reported regions. It does not reconstruct the clusters. The superior temporal gyrus, also named by both studies, is not added.

[Scharf et al. (2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10493383/) prospectively followed 37 patients with isolated thalamic infarcts. Four of the five anterior-territory patients reached the HADS anxiety screening threshold (≥ 8) at one month. Corrected voxel mapping placed the anxiety association in the left thalamus, over the ventral lateral and ventral medial nuclei. Most strokes were left-sided, and prestroke anxiety was not formally measured. The whole left thalamus is coloured as a coarse landmark; the anterior nuclear group is not substituted for the reported ventral nuclei.

[Liebermann et al. (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22854887/) studied 68 patients a mean 37 months after thalamic stroke. The 11 patients whose lesions involved the posterior thalamus reported more emotional disturbance and anxiety than patients with more anterior lesions. The whole cohort did not differ from 34 transient-ischaemic-attack controls, and lesion side had no effect. The authors proposed damage to medial-pulvinar connections as a mechanism. This study adds no coloured region (see corrections below).

## Reduplicative paramnesia for places

This profile covers the acquired, persistent belief that a place has been duplicated, relocated or merged with another, despite clear counterevidence. It is distinct from navigational disorientation and from misidentification of people, such as [Fregoli syndrome](deficit-additions-2026-11-07-hart-further.md).

[Carlisle et al. (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34144999/) describe an 81-year-old woman after a large right middle cerebral artery infarct. She insisted that the hospital was her home in another city, and pointed out where her kitchen, bathroom, living room and front door were. The diffusion-imaging caption and discussion name the right inferior frontal gyrus, insula, precentral gyrus and supramarginal gyrus, and to a lesser extent the internal capsule and lentiform nucleus. Only the precentral and supramarginal gyri are coloured. The report does not say which inferior frontal or insular subdivisions were injured, so those parcels stay uncoloured. A single large infarct cannot isolate the causal region.

[Diamantaras et al. (2023)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37515831/) compared 11 patients with reduplicative paramnesia with 11 patients with spatial disorientation. The patients were selected from the clinical records of the Bern and Geneva university hospitals and had mixed etiologies. A right dorsolateral prefrontal peak (BA46) was lesioned in seven cases and was the only case-specific region in the lesion comparison. Executive impairment was more frequent in tested cases than in controls. The result reports a Brodmann area and MNI coordinates rather than an atlas gyrus, so the finding stays uncoloured. The lesion-network nodes (bilateral anterior insula, right cingulate) are also uncoloured.

[Alves et al. (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33811370/) screened 400 acute right-hemisphere stroke patients and analysed 64 cases and 233 controls. The strongest predictor was structural disconnection along two distinct right-hemisphere streams, one fronto-thalamic and one occipitotemporal. Sparing of dorsal right frontoparietal tissue, age and anosognosia were independent predictors. [Alves et al. (2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10086811/) is a subanalysis of the same recruitment cohort, extended by 104 later patients. It characterized 60 cases whose belief persisted after counterevidence was shown systematically. The two reports are not independent replications. Spared tissue, estimated disconnection and normative networks are not coloured. Alves 2021 remains abstract-scope.

## Working memory, basal-forebrain amnesia and semantic control

[Barbey et al. (2013)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3495093/) found worse Letter–Number Sequencing in seven veterans with focal left dorsolateral prefrontal lesions than in healthy controls. The left group did not differ from 20 focal non-dorsolateral prefrontal cases. Forward spans, monitoring and N-back showed no reliable focal deficits. No multiple-comparison correction was reported, and the paper names Brodmann areas 9/46 rather than a gyrus, so the finding stays uncoloured. The cohort comes from the same Vietnam Head Injury Study registry as the catalogued [Koenigs et al. (2009)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2799248/) parietal study. Koenigs's discussion stated, without presenting the analysis, that 21 veterans with dorsolateral prefrontal lesions had no significant working-memory impairment. That statement is retained as qualifying evidence.

[Baier et al. (2010)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6632833/) mapped two contrasts in the same 61 acute stroke patients. A larger distractor cost was associated with left putamen and adjacent white-matter damage; the whole left putamen is coloured. A larger cost of raising memory load from three to five locations was associated with right frontal white matter, inferior frontal gyrus, caudate head and insula. The whole right caudate and insula are coloured. The inferior frontal gyrus is unsubdivided in the paper and stays uncoloured. These are two findings from one cohort, not independent replications.

[Abe et al. (1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9667575/) describe predominantly anterograde amnesia in a 61-year-old man after clipping of an unruptured anterior communicating artery aneurysm. The lesion was centred on the right diagonal band, with septal, hypothalamic and patchy contralateral injury. An earlier left external-capsule haemorrhage is reported. The right diagonal band is an orientation landmark only. [Mugikura et al. (2014)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7965321/) qualify it: in ten postoperative amnesic patients, infarcts spread across the subcallosal-artery territory and always involved the fornix columns, but involved the diagonal band in only five.

[Souter et al. (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9653334/) found some opercular inferior frontal injury in 16 of 23 patients with semantic aphasia. [Thompson et al. (2022)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36183573/) showed equivalent semantic-control difficulties in eight patients whose prefrontal cortex was spared. The left opercular inferior frontal parcel is a descriptive landmark for a commonly injured region, not a control-specific centre. In Souter's exploratory maps, semantic clusters fell in anterior inferior frontal cortex, and posterior inferior frontal clusters were associated with a nonverbal executive test. The studies share investigators and reuse earlier cases.

## Corrections made during verification

The third batch had been written into the catalogue without this review. Checking it against the sources changed the following:

- **Liebermann.** Its results could be read only in the abstract, and its methods in a partial author-posted copy. The earlier text said that descriptive lesion subtraction implicated the pulvinar, "especially on the right". Neither source shows that result, so the right-pulvinar mapping was removed. The finding now states the posterior-versus-anterior comparison and the authors' proposed medial-pulvinar mechanism. The reference scope changed from full-text to abstract, and a limitation no longer compares "HADS thresholds" that Liebermann's text does not report.
- **Knutson.** "Lesion volume differed" became the reported difference in brain-volume loss, and the population now reads "mainly penetrating".
- **Carlisle.** "Identified its rooms" became the rooms of her imagined home that she pointed out. "Inferior frontal cortex … and deeper structures" became the gyri and the less-affected deep structures that the report names.
- **Alves 2021.** "Disconnection linking" two systems became two distinct disconnection streams, and the other independent predictors were added.
- **Diamantaras.** "Retrospectively selected" became selection from clinical records, and "mixed etiologies" was restored in French.
- **Barbey.** The limitation no longer implies a corrected Letter–Number Sequencing result. The comparison group is described as non-dorsolateral, mainly ventromedial, prefrontal cases, and registry overlap with Koenigs is stated rather than called potential.
- **Koenigs.** "A differently selected 21-patient prefrontal subgroup" became the unpresented 21-veteran dorsolateral statement in its discussion. Because that claim needs the full text, the `koenigs-2009` scope changed from abstract to full-text. This is the only change to a reference record older than the Hart passes.
- **Baier.** Subgyral white matter is attributed to the prefrontal lesions, as the paper says, rather than to all lesions.
- **Mugikura.** The population is ten of 14 consecutive patients, selected for a memory quotient more than 15 points below IQ. The finding names the subcallosal-artery territory. The mapping note adds the five-in-ten diagonal-band involvement. The scope changed to full-text.
- **Souter.** The mapping note adds where the exploratory semantic and executive clusters fell. The French now keeps "some" injury.

## Provenance and verification

Fresh PubMed efetch records matched every title, author list, journal, issue year, DOI and PMID. Alves 2023 uses its 2023 issue year. All 14 citation-cache records were regenerated with `scripts/fetch_references.py` (`pubmed_articles`, `article_record` and its notice checks), and they equalled the records already merged. No correction, retraction or expression-of-concern notice was returned. The cache holds 425 records.

Eleven of the new references have full-text scope and three have abstract scope (Ku, Liebermann and Alves 2021). Full texts were read from PMC or from open-access, archived or author-posted copies: Carlisle, Diamantaras, Thompson and Abe. Every mapped region resolves on CDMD, and its `source_name` matches the manifest label. The scopes describe the extraction source, not independent numerical reproduction or clinical validation.

Independent read-only reviews checked each finding against its sources, and the corrections above apply their material points. The catalogue, paint and deficit-panel tests were updated to the new totals and identifiers. They guard the removed pulvinar mapping and the corrected scopes.

The search for "amnesia" now also returns reduplicative paramnesia, because "paramnesia" contains the query. Ties after an exact name sort alphabetically. This follows the catalogue's substring search and was kept. The selection is targeted, not systematic, and does not establish patient-level diagnostic or prognostic accuracy.
