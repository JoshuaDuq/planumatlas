# Deficit additions: memory, fluency, fear learning and moral judgement

Review date and catalogue revision: `2026-11-01`.

This pass adds five bilingual profiles, nine findings and 23 references, bringing the catalogue to 99 profiles, 156 findings and 219 references. All 94 earlier profiles, 147 findings, 196 references and their cached citation records retain their values. It follows the removal of profiles that coloured no atlas region, so every finding added here colours at least one region. The earlier linked reviews retain their historical scopes and counts.

## Inclusion decisions

Selection required peer-reviewed primary human patient evidence, a deficit distinct from the existing profiles, and complementary evidence from more than one sample. Each profile also had to name a location defensible as an atlas landmark. Material-specific memory differs from global amnesia by its lateralized, material-dependent pattern. Semantic memory impairment concerns stored conceptual knowledge, not the naming errors already covered under aphasia. Verbal fluency is a timed generation task distinct from picture naming. Fear conditioning concerns acquiring a bodily response, not recognizing emotion in faces or voices. Moral judgement concerns evaluating harm in scenarios, not the gambling-task decisions already profiled.

This is a targeted content review, not a systematic review or independent specialist assessment. Meta-analyses are cited for their pooled estimates without re-deriving them. Papers from the same registry or laboratory are not treated as independent replications.

## Source identity and extraction scope

The 23 PMIDs, titles, author lists, issue years and DOIs were fetched with the existing PubMed/Crossref reference-checking code and merged into the reference cache without altering earlier records. No linked correction, retraction or expression of concern was returned. Stuss et al. (1998) has no DOI in PubMed; Crossref's DOI was accepted only after the code matched its title and first author. Thomas et al. (2011) has a 2011 issue year despite its 2010 online record.

All 23 references use abstract scope. Selected main-text passages were consulted for four open-access articles. These covered participants, lesion analysis, results and stated limitations. The consultation does not upgrade them to full-text extraction.

- Young et al. (2010): nine bilateral ventromedial patients, five of whom were in Koenigs et al. (2007). Seven brain-damaged and eight healthy comparison participants were recruited. Patients judged attempted harms more permissible than accidental harms but still distinguished harmful from neutral intentions in other conditions.
- Thomas et al. (2011): nine bilateral ventromedial patients, six after orbitofrontal meningioma resection and three after anterior communicating artery aneurysm rupture. Nine brain-damaged and 11 healthy comparison participants took part.
- Biesbroek et al. (2016): voxelwise results at a false-discovery-rate threshold of q = 0.05. The authors note low left-hemisphere lesion frequency because patients with severe aphasia were often not tested.
- Rouse et al. (2024): left and right resection groups were both mildly impaired, with no left–right differences in general semantic processing.

Biesbroek et al. (2016) name the phonemic-only frontal region as the middle frontal gyrus in the abstract but as the medial frontal gyrus in the region-of-interest results. The letter-fluency middle frontal mapping therefore rests on the 1,231-patient study.

The catalogue now contains 64 full-text and 155 abstract-based reference records.

## Screened evidence not imported

Jefferies & Lambon Ralph (2006, PMID 16815878) distinguish semantic control deficits in stroke aphasia from the degraded representations of semantic dementia. Its abstract describes lesions only as prefrontal or temporoparietal. A semantic-control finding would also overlap the existing aphasia findings, so none was added.

Smith & Milner (1981, PMID 7329524) has no PubMed abstract and was not cited; their 1989 follow-up, which has one, is used instead.

LaBar et al. (1995) is available in PubMed Central only as page scans, and no participant count was extracted. The population records "the abstract gives no count" rather than a number recalled from elsewhere.

## Verification

Catalogue checks validate bilingual content, citation identities, atlas source labels and mappings for the published anatomy. Targeted tests fix each new profile's coloured regions. They keep the parahippocampal cortex out of the spatial-memory finding and the rolandic operculum out of verbal fluency. They also check that each profile ranks first for a distinct English or French search term. A baseline comparison confirmed that every earlier catalogue entry and cached citation record is unchanged. These checks establish integration and provenance, not diagnostic or prognostic performance.
