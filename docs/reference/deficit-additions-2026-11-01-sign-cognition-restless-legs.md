# Deficit additions: sign language aphasia, post-stroke cognitive impairment and restless legs

Review date and catalogue revision: `2026-11-01`.

This pass adds three bilingual profiles, five findings and 11 references, bringing the catalogue to 114 profiles, 178 findings and 271 references. All 111 earlier profiles, 173 findings, 260 references and their cached citation records retain their values. The preceding [cardiac autonomic dysfunction, hiccups and motor adaptation review](deficit-additions-2026-11-01-cardiac-hiccups-adaptation.md) and earlier linked reviews retain their historical scopes and counts.

## Inclusion decisions

Selection required peer-reviewed primary human patient evidence and complementary evidence from more than one research group. Each profile also had to have at least one finding naming a location defensible as an atlas landmark. Sign language aphasia is profiled separately from aphasia because its population and modality differ and it tests whether left-hemisphere dominance depends on auditory processing. Post-stroke cognitive impairment is a global outcome measure; it complements, rather than replaces, the domain-specific profiles. Restless legs after stroke is a sleep-related movement disorder distinct from the existing hypersomnia profile.

This is a targeted content review, not a systematic review or independent specialist assessment. Studies from one programme or consortium are not treated as independent replications.

## Source identity and extraction scope

The 11 PMIDs, titles, author lists, issue years and DOIs were fetched with the existing PubMed/Crossref reference-checking code and merged into the reference cache without altering earlier records. Author fields were filled from the fetched records. Weaver et al. (2021) is the only work with a linked notice. Its correction was read in its PubMed Central copy and is recorded in the catalogue and the cache. No retraction or expression of concern was returned.

All 11 references use abstract scope. The catalogue now contains 64 full-text and 207 abstract-based reference records.

## Screened evidence not imported

- **Antisaccade errors after frontal lesions.** [Pierrot-Deseilligny et al. (2003)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12764065/) describe three patients with dorsolateral prefrontal lesions, and Gaymard et al. (1999) one frontal eye field case; both come from one Paris group. The independent study by Hodgson et al. (2007, PMID 17452376) has an erratum that could not be resolved to a DOI or read. It was therefore not cited, leaving one group's evidence.
- **Responses to unfair offers after ventromedial prefrontal damage.** [Koenigs & Tranel (2007)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17251437/) found patients rejected unfair offers more often. [Gu et al. (2015)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25589742/) found them more likely to accept the most unfair offers. Two small studies with opposite directions do not support a coherent deficit profile.

## Verification

Targeted tests fix each finding's coloured regions, keep the right-hemisphere sign language finding uncoloured, and check Weaver's recorded correction DOI. Each profile is tested to rank first for a distinct search term. The existing tests for the left middle temporal region and the "aphas" search now include sign language aphasia. During integration, the catalogue validator caught a wrong atlas label for the left thalamus, which was corrected to `Left-Thalamus-Proper`. A baseline comparison confirmed that every earlier catalogue entry and cached citation record is unchanged. These checks establish integration and provenance, not diagnostic or prognostic performance.
