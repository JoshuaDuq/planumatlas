# Deficit additions: pantomime, tool use, deep lesions and callosal apraxia

Review date and catalogue revision: `2026-11-02`.

This pass adds one bilingual profile, five findings and 13 references, bringing the catalogue to 121 profiles, 193 findings and 305 references. All 120 earlier profiles, 188 findings, 292 references and their cached citation records retain their values; the limb apraxia profile's summary and search terms were widened to cover its new findings. The preceding [supplementary motor area, recognition memory and action naming review](deficit-additions-2026-11-01-sma-recognition-verbs.md) and earlier linked reviews retain their historical scopes and counts.

## Source of the candidates

The candidates come from the praxis chapter of John Hart Jr., *The Neurobiology of Cognition and Behavior* (Oxford University Press, 2016), printed pp. 77–87. Before this pass the Praxis domain held only meaningless hand and finger imitation. The chapter also covers pantomime to command, conceptual errors with real objects, apraxia after deep and callosal lesions, dissociation apraxia and limb-kinetic apraxia. The book was used only to find candidates. Each claim imported here rests on the primary studies, which were found and read through PubMed E-utilities, and on competing studies the chapter does not cite.

## Inclusion decisions

Selection followed the earlier passes: peer-reviewed primary human patient evidence, complementary evidence from more than one research group, and a location defensible as an atlas landmark. Every limb apraxia finding also carries qualifying evidence, as the catalogue's tests require.

This is a targeted content review, not a systematic review or independent specialist assessment. Studies from one group are not treated as independent replications: Hoeren and Martin share the Freiburg group, so each is paired with a study from another centre. Pramstaller and Marsden's review may include De Renzi's patients.

## Candidates not imported

- **Ideomotor apraxia after supplementary motor area injury.** Watson et al. (1986) report two patients whose infarcts included but were not confined to the supplementary motor area. It is used only to qualify the callosal finding.
- **Dissociation apraxia.** Modality-specific cases exist, but the chapter itself states that their lesion locations are unclear.
- **Limb-kinetic apraxia.** The chapter cites no primary lesion study for its premotor claims. Pramstaller and Marsden argue that the term still needs quantified deficits.
- **Learned motor sequences.** Dovern et al. (2011) relate dorsal premotor damage to retrieving a learned sequence, an experimental learning task rather than a clinical apraxia measure.
- **Marchiafava-Bignami disease.** Berek et al. (1994) is a single case and adds no location beyond the callosal reports.

The chapter says only three of De Renzi et al.'s 14 patients were impaired. The PubMed abstract reports five severely impaired at imitation and pantomime: two primarily thalamic and three lenticular with posterior internal capsule involvement. The catalogue follows the primary abstract.

## Source identity and extraction scope

The 13 PMIDs, titles, author lists, issue years and DOIs were fetched with the existing PubMed/Crossref reference-checking code. They were merged into the reference cache without refetching or altering earlier records. No linked correction, retraction or expression of concern was returned. Buxbaum et al. (2014) uses full-text scope, from its results section and Table 2 on PubMed Central; the other 12 use abstract scope. The catalogue now contains 65 full-text and 240 abstract-based reference records.

## Verification

Targeted tests fix each new finding's coloured regions, including the empty paint of the deep-lesion finding and the absence of the splenium from callosal apraxia. They confirm each opposing study stays as qualifying evidence. They also check that the limb and callosal profiles rank first for distinct English and French search terms. Two region expectations grew: the left opercular inferior frontal and left middle temporal lists now include the pantomime findings. These checks establish integration and provenance, not diagnostic or prognostic performance.
