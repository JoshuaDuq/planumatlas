# Hart (2016): targeted functions and deficits review

Reviewed 2 November 2026 against John Hart Jr., *The Neurobiology of Cognition and Behavior* (Oxford University Press, 2016), ISBN 9780190219031. Printed book page numbers are given below. This is a targeted comparison, not a systematic review or a verification of every claim in the 264-page PDF. Text extraction from the scanned PDF is imperfect; the cited passages were checked in context.

## Changes made

- Language (pp. 51–56): broaden the aphasia introduction and describe the repetition result as an association in the pooled analysis, not a necessary lesion in every patient. The underlying source is Na et al. (2022), PMID 35569227.
- Memory (p. 95; p. 178): introduce both anterograde and retrograde loss and note structures and connections beyond the hippocampus; the existing separate findings retain their original evidence and mappings.
- Praxis (pp. 77–78): distinguish gesture imitation, command and tool use without treating the imitation findings as a map of all praxis.
- Visual loss: remove cortical blindness and quadrantanopia as aliases of homonymous hemianopia; they describe related but distinct patterns. The original hemianopia finding remains unchanged.
- Functions (p. 213): make the distinction between activation consistency, functional specificity and lesion consequences visible on function profiles. The overview combines term maps; see the atlas methods for the uniformity statistic and aggregation.

## Praxis additions

The praxis chapter (pp. 77–87) was then used to find candidates for extending the Praxis domain. The added findings rest on primary studies verified through PubMed, not on the book: pantomime of tool use (frontal and parietotemporal findings kept apart), real tool use, deep lesions (uncoloured) and a new callosal apraxia profile. Candidates not imported and one discrepancy with the book's account of De Renzi et al. (1986) are recorded in the [praxis additions review](deficit-additions-2026-11-02-praxis.md).

## Second-pass corrections

A chapter-by-chapter pass (pp. 43–219) found these errors in existing wording. Each was checked against the primary abstracts, not the book.

- **Korsakoff amnesia** (pp. 95–97): the limitation said the mammillary bodies mark the disease rather than the amnesia, contradicting the map's own recollection finding (Vann et al. 2009, PMID 19289844). It now says mammillary damage did not separate the groups in Harding et al. (2000) without excluding it, with Vann as qualifying evidence; the mapping note names the uncoloured mediodorsal nucleus.
- **Simultanagnosia** (p. 113): renamed from "Dorsal simultanagnosia". Chechlacz et al. (2012), PMID 22066584, did not classify subtypes; the limitation now says its lesions match the dorsal pattern, and the finding lists the other sites its abstract names.
- **Pure alexia** (pp. 176–177): Epelbaum et al. (2008), PMID 18586235, a later diffusion study by the same group that reviews treat as the Gaillard et al. (2006) patient, found the word form area intact and the inferior longitudinal fasciculus degenerating. It qualifies the surgical finding.
- **Tactile agnosia** (p. 130): the mapping note now states that the agnosia affected the hand opposite the lesion.
- **Aliases**: disinhibition removed from abulia (pp. 166, 182); acquired dyslexia added to phonological and surface alexia (pp. 64–65); Bálint syndrome given to both its mapped components, simultanagnosia and optic ataxia (p. 114); Broca's aphasia added to aphasia so the term no longer leads only to apraxia of speech (p. 54).

Errors in the book's own citations found in this pass, not to be imported: Halsband et al. (2001) on gesture recognition, Chechlacz et al. (2012) on egocentric neglect, Samuelsson et al. (1998) on vigilance, Pitel et al. (2008) on working memory in Korsakoff patients, Dronkers et al. (2007) on the insula, and Riddoch et al. (2008) on integrative agnosia.

## Additional comparisons

- **Optic ataxia** (p. 114): the book emphasizes extensive bilateral posterior parietal injury. The map's Karnath and Perenin (2005), PMID 15716470, reports unilateral cases and a junction-centred overlap. Retain the map's task and selection caveats rather than substituting the book's simplified localization.
- **Pure word deafness** (pp. 123–125): the book discusses bilateral and less common unilateral lesions. The existing profile combines a bilateral cortical case with a qualifying unilateral case and does not require bilateral destruction; no mapping change warranted on this comparison.
- **Executive control** (pp. 135–139): the book discusses task-dependent and differing frontal associations. The existing profile already cautions that tests and subregions differ and uses broad landmarks, not a single executive center.
- **Working memory** (pp. 150–152): the book distinguishes manipulation from simpler retention and notes white-matter involvement. The existing Koenigs finding distinguishes tasks, describes underlying connections and includes a separate precision study; its bilateral display does not imply bilateral lesions.
- **Mood** (pp. 157–158): the book presents historical hemispheric accounts and a summary table. The existing post-stroke depressive-symptoms profile avoids a fixed left-hemisphere rule and distinguishes symptom scales from diagnosis. The book alone does not justify new location or laterality claims.
- **Networks and white matter** (pp. 169–183, 187–188, 207, 213): the book discusses disconnection and networks, while also warning that association does not establish necessity. Existing mapping notes and function-map cautions preserve these distinctions. Tractography or healthy-connectome associations should not be presented as direct damage without separate evidence.

The book is a 2016 secondary source and several accounts are explicitly provisional. Further candidate additions would need independent review of the primary studies, competing findings, population, task and atlas resolution; this comparison does not establish clinical or diagnostic validity.
