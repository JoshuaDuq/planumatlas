## Acquired obsessive-compulsive symptoms

[Kim and Lee (2002)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11884667/) describe new checking rituals in a 66-year-old man with a left medial orbitofrontal infarct, explicitly involving the gyrus rectus and medial orbital gyrus. Infarct timing was uncertain. [Ogai et al. (2005)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15636597/) provide an independent right orbitofrontal contusion case after head injury. The latter qualifies an exclusively left-sided interpretation; its abstract does not justify a precise right parcel.

The direct-injury finding colours only the whole left gyrus rectus as a landmark for part of Kim's lesion. Two selected reports cannot establish risk, necessity or sufficiency. The profile is named **acquired obsessive-compulsive symptoms**, without presenting it as a lesion map of idiopathic OCD.

The final published [Cotovio et al. (2026)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42119759/) study identified 129 published cases, with 40 traced lesions compared with 608 control lesions and normative connectivity from 1,000 participants. Its main-text discussion distinguishes more robust connectivity findings from lesion-topography findings that were less robust to potential confounders. Orbitofrontal and ventral basal-ganglia connections therefore remain a separate, uncoloured finding. These literature cases may include the original reports; their counts are not pooled as independent patients. The inspected main text did not establish adult-only eligibility for the pooled sample.

Kim's [published correction](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/full/10.1176/jnp.14.2.242) restores an omitted author affiliation. Its DOI and bilingual explanation are recorded; it changes no clinical or anatomical findings. Kim's complete letter/correction and Cotovio's final main-text methods, results and discussion were inspected. Ogai remains abstract-scope. The final 2026 article, rather than a preprint, supplies the network finding; its supplements were not obtained. Berthier's unresolved correction and Chacko's less temporally resolved case series were not needed for this inclusion decision.

## Klüver–Bucy syndrome

[Hayman et al. (1998)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9706545/) report a 43-year-old man with bilateral amygdala, temporal and insular abnormalities during melanoma treatment after correction of severe hyponatremia. [Park et al. (2012)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3952319/) report a 31-year-old man after encephalitis and prolonged status epilepticus, with bilateral hippocampal swelling followed by atrophy and persistent cognitive/behavioral changes. These are complementary adult structural cases, with different clinical and imaging limitations.

Two separate findings colour whole bilateral amygdalae and whole bilateral hippocampi. They illustrate reported involved structures; they do not establish that either pair alone is necessary or sufficient, or that all four structures must be damaged together. Hayman's injury was broader than the amygdala and of uncertain etiology. Park's authors could not exclude MRI-invisible microscopic or functional injury elsewhere. The atlas does not claim clinical-MRI precision for basolateral amygdala or CA1 subdivisions.

Hayman's publisher-indexed case account, discussion and figure caption were inspected, without independently inspecting the original MRI image. Park's full text and original figure were inspected. Both references have full-text extraction scope; neither is a controlled lesion comparison. Reduced emotional responsiveness/placidity is retained as part of the classic phenotype. The textbook's hyperaggressiveness description is not substituted for it, although individual patients can have variable behavior.

## Impaired cognitive estimation

This profile concerns plausible numerical estimates of everyday quantities when the exact answer is unknown. It is distinct from calculation impairment, general reasoning and interval timing.

[Pflugshaupt et al. (2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667527/) studied 55 right-stroke patients with left neglect and 55 controls. The patient group made less plausible estimates without the predicted general bias towards large magnitudes. Corrected region-based associations included right superior temporal pole and orbital inferior frontal gyrus, as well as white matter. Those two cortical parcels are coloured as landmarks; the uncinate fasciculus and anterior commissure are uncoloured. The selected right-sided cohort cannot establish exclusive right lateralization, and its lesion coverage favoured middle cerebral artery injury.

[Cipolotti et al. (2018)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6018564/) found impaired estimation after frontal stroke/tumour lesions relative to posterior-lesion patients and controls. The difference from healthy controls persisted after adjustment for arithmetic and fluid-intelligence scores. A separate uncoloured finding retains this broad frontal result because the imaging subset did not permit precise anatomical inference. The right-stroke study qualifies a frontal-exclusive interpretation.

Both full texts were inspected. Cipolotti uses the **2018 journal-issue year**, despite its 2017 online date and DOI. Its abstract reports 38 frontal and 22 posterior patients; final methods and Table 1 report 34 and 19. The population field explicitly records this discrepancy. It is not silently reconciled or described as a published correction; the identity/notice checks returned no correction notice.

## Fregoli syndrome

[Kakegawa et al. (2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7667346/) detected ten Fregoli cases among 874 consecutive first-stroke rehabilitation admissions. All ten had right lesions and left neglect. Their comparison with 24 right-stroke/left-neglect controls found a broad right frontotemporal, subcortical and white-matter pattern, peaking in anterior-inferior insula and adjacent white matter.

Only the short right insular parcel is coloured, as an anterior-insular landmark. It does not reproduce the voxel peak, show the entire associated pattern or map every subtype of delusional misidentification. Cases had lower global cognitive scores and more pusher syndrome than controls. Spontaneous-speech monitoring could miss cases, and no Capgras case was detected; the reported rehabilitation frequency is not a general population estimate. These cases involved persistent, nonthreatening acquaintance misidentification, differing from the full classical disguise presentation; that distinction is explicit in the profile.

[Darby et al. (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28082298/) supply complementary, broader lesion-network evidence across 17 published misidentification cases. Their connected retrosplenial and frontal nodes remain uncoloured and are not described as direct injury sites. This mixed-syndrome analysis is not an independent replication of Kakegawa's Fregoli-specific insular result. Kakegawa has full-text scope; Darby remains abstract-scope because its abstract supplies the retained connectivity claims.
