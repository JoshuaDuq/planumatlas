# Hart (2016): further deficit candidates

Review date: 7 November 2026. **Review only: no profiles, findings, references or mappings have been added to the application.**

## Decision

The book identifies useful gaps, but does not by itself justify new lesion maps. Four candidates deserve the next catalogue-writing pass: **object-colour knowledge impairment, impaired spatial-relation judgement, acquired mania, and visual anosognosia (Anton syndrome)**. Each has patient evidence beyond the textbook and a possible anatomical landmark. The proposed landmarks below still require finding-specific wording and catalogue validation before use.

Acquired obsessive-compulsive symptoms and Klüver–Bucy syndrome are additional candidates, with unresolved source or mapping work. Their clinical existence is better established than a simple, specific parcel map.

## Scope and comparison

The supplied source is John Hart Jr., _The Neurobiology of Cognition and Behavior_ (Oxford University Press, 2016), ISBN 9780190219031. The PDF has 264 pages. Page references below are **printed book pages**; for these passages, PDF page number is printed page number plus 18.

The comparison used the current `data/neuropsychology.yaml`: revision 2026-11-02, 121 profiles, 193 findings and 306 references. Profile names, summaries, aliases and findings were considered, not just literal name matches. The existing [Hart review](hart-2016-review-2026-11-02.md) and [praxis additions](deficit-additions-2026-11-02-praxis.md) were also consulted.

This is a targeted content review. Clinical chapters and relevant white-matter/network passages were screened using the extracted text; candidate passages were examined in context, including rendered scans. It is not a verification of every sentence, illustration or bibliography entry in the book, a systematic literature review, or an independent specialist assessment. Twenty-one candidate-related PubMed records were retrieved through E-utilities to check titles, years, identifiers, abstracts and linked notices. Full-text passages were inspected where stated below. No patient images or lesion masks were independently reanalysed.

The existing inclusion rules require a distinguishable acquired adult impairment, primary patient evidence with complementary publications, and at least one defensible atlas mapping per profile. Healthy activation, an isolated case alone, or a connected network node does not satisfy these requirements.

## Candidates for the next writing pass

### 1. Object-colour knowledge impairment

**Book:** p. 116, explicitly distinguished from impaired colour perception. **Suggested profile:** `object-colour-knowledge`, perception group; cross-link to semantic memory.

[Miceli et al. (2001), PMID 11369950](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11369950/) describe two patients with impaired object-colour knowledge despite preserved colour perception and naming; one also retained knowledge of form, size and function. Their abstract supplies only a broad left mesial temporal localization.

[Stasenko et al. (2014), PMID 25058612](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4135534/) provide an independent adult stroke case with preserved colour perception and naming. The case description and experimental results support the distinction. Injury included left lingual and medial fusiform cortex, substantial white matter and bilateral cingulate-isthmus damage; alexia and naming problems coexisted.

**Possible map:** left fusiform and lingual parcels as landmarks in that case, with an explicit multiregion-lesion limitation. Do not present either gyrus as an isolated colour-knowledge centre. This is not an alias of achromatopsia or merely difficulty naming colour patches. The current semantic-memory profile concerns broader conceptual impairment and does not describe this dissociation.

**Reading scope:** Miceli abstract; Stasenko full-text clinical, task/result and discussion passages.

### 2. Impaired spatial-relation judgement

**Book:** pp. 200–201. **Suggested profile:** `spatial-relations`, perception group, with categorical and metric tasks distinguished.

[Amorapanth et al. (2010), PMID 19642889](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2933471/) studied 34 chronic unilateral-lesion patients and 12 older controls. Categorical picture matching implicated left frontal and inferior parietal regions; metric matching implicated right middle temporal cortex. Residual analyses make the hemispheric distinction more nuanced than a left/right rule. The relevant patient methods and results, including permutation correction, were inspected.

[Tranel and Kemmerer (2004), PMID 21038229](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21038229/) studied locative-preposition knowledge in 78 lesion patients and 60 controls. Left frontal opercular and supramarginal involvement provides complementary evidence. Its language tasks differ from nonverbal matching; the focused six-patient analysis is not an additional independent cohort.

**Possible map:** left angular/supramarginal landmarks for categorical-task associations; a separate right middle temporal finding for metric matching. Do not call all mapped cortex uniquely categorical or infer a cortical lesion from subjacent white-matter damage. Existing left–right disorientation, line-orientation, navigation and object-location-memory profiles assess different tasks.

**Reading scope:** Amorapanth relevant full-text methods/results/discussion; Tranel abstract. Complementary evidence, not an exact replication.

### 3. Acquired mania after brain injury

**Book:** pp. 161–162. **Suggested profile:** `acquired-mania`, social-emotional group; distinguish it from behavioral disinhibition and primary bipolar disorder.

[Cummings and Mendez (1984), PMID 6465386](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6465386/) describe two patients with mania after right thalamic infarction. This supports a case-series finding mapped to the right whole thalamus; it does not identify a particular thalamic nucleus.

[Cotovio et al. (2020), PMID 32831292](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32831292/) analysed 41 literature lesions and an independent 15-patient clinical cohort. Locations were heterogeneous. Their right orbitofrontal, inferior temporal and frontal-pole results concern normative connectivity, so those nodes must not be painted as directly damaged sites on that basis.

[Barahona-Corrêa et al. (2020), PMID 32398699](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32398699/) supply a pooled lesion analysis supporting right-sided predominance, with left-sided and bilateral exceptions. Published-case selection and possible overlap with the other literature samples preclude pooling their counts as independent observations.

**Decision:** a supported candidate with a deliberately narrow right-thalamic case finding and separate network evidence. Avoid a statement that right-sided damage invariably produces mania, or that all cases require right-sided damage.

**Reading scope:** fetched abstracts for these three studies.

### 4. Visual anosognosia / Anton syndrome

**Book:** p. 179. **Suggested profile:** `visual-anosognosia`, attention-executive group, searchable as Anton syndrome.

[Chaudhry et al. (2019), PMID 31801772](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31801772/) describe four adults with visual anosognosia and bilateral occipital injury, sometimes with additional damage. A [2021 case report, PMID 36324705](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36324705/), describes bilateral occipital infarcts and right frontal/parietal hemorrhagic injury. Its [2022 correction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9616237/) supplies omitted consent/ethical statements; it does not report a numerical or localization correction.

[Monai et al. (2024), PMID 37706575](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ana.26791) compared 16 literature cases with visual controls whose unilateral lesions were mirrored to form synthetic bilateral controls. Direct damage and atlas-predicted disconnection must remain separate. This design does not establish that a single cortical parcel produces unawareness. The publication's issue year is 2024 despite online publication in 2023.

**Possible map:** bilateral calcarine parcels can orient the reader to the striate component of the reported injury, but cannot stand for the full syndrome or its broader extrastriate lesions. Frontoparietal connectivity findings should remain uncoloured unless separately supported as direct injury. Published cases can overlap across these sources.

**Decision:** distinct from hemianopia, motor anosognosia and spontaneous confabulation. Cortical blindness can occur with awareness; it must not be treated as synonymous with Anton syndrome.

**Reading scope:** clinical abstracts; Monai full-text methods, lesion/disconnection results and limitations; correction text.

## Additional candidates needing further source or mapping work

### Acquired obsessive-compulsive symptoms

**Book:** pp. 162–163. This is absent as a distinct profile and is not equivalent to executive perseveration, reversal-learning difficulty or disinhibition.

[Chacko et al. (2000), PMID 11001608](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/full/10.1176/jnp.12.2.269) describe five later-life cases with basal-ganglia abnormalities. Relevant clinical and limitation passages show important uncertainty about lesion timing/etiology and coexisting depression. A right caudate lesion in one case coexisted with other cortical lesions; the series cannot justify painting both caudates as the universal substrate.

[Cotovio et al. (2026), PMID 42119759](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42119759/) is a published study, replacing its 2025 preprint for citation purposes. Its abstract reports 129 lesion-associated cases, 40 clearly localized lesions and 608 lesion controls, with orbitofrontal/right temporal-pole overlap and separate connectivity results. The final full methods/results and robustness analyses need review before choosing exact parcels. The abstract was verified through PubMed; this review does not substitute the preprint's details for the final article.

[Berthier et al. (1996), PMID 8757004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8757004/) describe 13 cases and carry a linked erratum, _Neurology_ 47(3):855. The existing automated checker could not resolve that notice to a unique DOI. Its contents remain unverified, so this reference is **not cleared for catalogue import**.

**Decision:** retain as a serious candidate; finish final-article and correction review before a mapped addition. Do not derive a lesion map from treatment-related metabolic changes or leukotomy response.

### Klüver–Bucy syndrome

**Book:** pp. 163–164. This combined syndrome is absent even though some component deficits are covered.

[Marlowe et al. (1975), PMID 168031](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/168031/) describe an adult after meningoencephalitis with bilateral temporal damage, reduced emotional responsiveness, oral exploration, altered sexual/feeding behavior, agnosia, aphasia and amnesia. [Caro and Jimenez (2016), PMID 27380585](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27380585/) report two partial-syndrome cases and discuss structural damage and transient postictal dysfunction. These mechanisms are not interchangeable.

**Source discrepancy:** the scanned book really says “hyperaggressiveness” on p. 163; this is not merely an extraction error. The checked clinical sources instead describe placidity or reduced emotional responsiveness among the characteristic features. Aggression should not be imported as a defining feature from this sentence.

**Decision:** retain for a dedicated review of adult structural cases and exact involved regions. Do not automatically paint the amygdalae or claim that bilateral amygdala destruction is required or sufficient. Broad temporal-lobe descriptions alone do not establish a particular hippocampal/amygdalar parcel map.

**Reading scope:** primary abstracts and the Caro article's opening explanatory passages; not a complete review of its patient accounts or the original lesion images.

## Verification and remaining limits

- Confirmed the current catalogue counts and checked that the proposed profiles are absent as distinct profiles. Related constructs and aliases were checked to avoid claiming that their entire domains are missing.
- Checked proposed landmark identifiers and source labels against `public/models/cdmd/manifest.json`: left fusiform `destrieux:left:21` (`G_oc-temp_lat-fusifor`), left lingual `destrieux:left:22` (`G_oc-temp_med-Lingual`), left angular `destrieux:left:25` (`G_pariet_inf-Angular`), left supramarginal `destrieux:left:26` (`G_pariet_inf-Supramar`), right middle temporal `destrieux:right:38` (`G_temporal_middle`), bilateral calcarine `destrieux:left:45` / `destrieux:right:45` (`S_calcarine`), and right thalamus `aseg:right:49` (`Right-Thalamus-Proper`). Identifier existence does not establish scientific specificity.
- Retrieved PubMed identities and linked-notice metadata for 21 records. Detected two linked errata: the Anton consent/ethics notice was read; the Berthier notice remains unresolved. A complete Crossref correction audit and the application's reference-cache import have not been completed. No statement of “all references cleared” is warranted.
- Checked complete rendered candidate pages to distinguish scan text from extraction errors. The hyperaggressiveness statement is visible in the printed source.
- No application content or existing user changes were modified. Application tests were not run because the only deliverable is this review document. No catalogue, bilingual text or proposed mapping has passed an implementation review in this task.

**Recommended order:** prepare the four principal candidates as separate, carefully qualified findings; complete the remaining OCD and Klüver–Bucy source work before deciding their maps; then expand existing working-memory and semantic-memory profiles. This is a content recommendation, not a claim of patient-level diagnostic validity.
