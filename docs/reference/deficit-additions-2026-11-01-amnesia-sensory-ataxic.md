Review date and catalogue revision: `2026-11-01`.

This pass adds three bilingual profiles, four findings and 10 references, bringing the catalogue to 117 profiles, 182 findings and 281 references. All 114 earlier profiles, 178 findings, 271 references and their cached citation records retain their values. The preceding [sign language aphasia, post-stroke cognitive impairment and restless legs review](deficit-additions-2026-11-01-sign-cognition-restless-legs.md) and earlier linked reviews retain their historical scopes and counts.

## Inclusion decisions

Selection required peer-reviewed primary human patient evidence and complementary evidence from more than one research group. Each profile also had to name a location defensible as an atlas landmark.

- **Transient global amnesia** is distinct from amnesia: it is a self-limited episode with full recovery, and its hippocampal lesions mark the episode rather than establishing a stroke mechanism.
- **Pure sensory stroke** is a lacunar syndrome, usually thalamic, distinct from the cortical upper-limb somatosensory profile and from crossed medullary sensory loss.
- **Ataxic hemiparesis** combines corticospinal and cerebellar signs after small deep strokes, distinct from cerebellar ataxia.

This is a targeted content review, not a systematic review or independent specialist assessment.

## Source identity and extraction scope

The 10 PMIDs, titles, author lists, issue years and DOIs were fetched with the existing PubMed/Crossref reference-checking code and merged into the reference cache without altering earlier records. Author fields were filled from the fetched records. No linked correction, retraction or expression of concern was returned. All 10 references use abstract scope. The catalogue now contains 64 full-text and 217 abstract-based reference records.

## Screened evidence not imported

- **Delusional misidentification (Capgras and reduplication).** [Darby et al. (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28082298/) localize 17 cases by lesion network mapping with normative connectivity. The catalogue keeps normative connectivity out of direct-lesion paint. [Devinsky (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19122035/) is a review rather than primary data, so no directly mappable primary series remained.
- **Thalamic aphasia.** [Fritsch et al. (2020)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31562559/) and [Rangus et al. (2022)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34100990/) come from one centre and appear to report the same 52 patients with isolated thalamic infarcts. The only independent evidence found was two cases ([Raymer et al., 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9025124/)), so the profile was deferred.
