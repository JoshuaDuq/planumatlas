## Inclusion decisions

Selection required human patient evidence in peer-reviewed primary research, a distinguishable clinical phenotype, and complementary evidence supporting or qualifying its inclusion. These profiles separate regularization errors from phonological decoding and pure alexia; attention to one's own body from external-space neglect and limb disownership; actual object use from gesture imitation; and tactile or auditory motion judgments from basic detection, object recognition or stationary sound localization. They describe selected acquired impairments, with varying evidence strength. A small surgical case series supports a narrowly described dissociation, not a population frequency or a universal lesion requirement. This is a targeted content review, not a systematic review or an independent specialist assessment.

The studies are not assumed to contain independent patient samples, and their counts are not pooled. Complementary peripheral-nerve evidence for tactile direction discrimination supports the test phenotype and an alternative explanation; it is not a second stroke-localization study.

## Source identity and extraction scope

The 12 included PMIDs, titles, author lists, issue years and DOIs were checked with the existing PubMed/Crossref reference-checking code. No linked correction, retraction or expression of concern was returned for the selected works. Committeri's DOI is `10.1093/brain/awl265`. Cocchini's issue year is 2022 despite its 2020 online-first record; Rumiati's issue year is 2001 despite later PubMed indexing. Only the 12 selected works were merged into the reference cache, preserving earlier records. The cache's `fetched` date describes incremental verification, not a complete new retrieval of older citations.

Three references use full-text scope. This denotes consultation of the main article beyond its abstract, with the extraction areas described below. No lesion masks, image pixels or supplemental datasets were independently reanalyzed.

For Binder, the participant and reading/semantic methods, lesion analyses, Results, principal tables and figure captions, and relevant Discussion and limitations were read. Participants were native English speakers at least 180 days after left stroke, with at least 10% single-word reading accuracy. Regularization errors were scored on irregular words; their corrected lesion result is distinguished from the negative corrected overall irregular-word-accuracy analysis after nonword adjustment. The posterior middle temporal result extends beyond one parcel. The semantic tests and poor anterior temporal coverage do not settle competing reading models or establish universally preserved semantics.

For Bertagnoli, the clinical and imaging methods, continuous-score regression analyses, Results, Discussion and limitations were read. Lesions were derived from 97 CT and seven MRI examinations; some clinical measures were imputed. The adjusted findings concern Heschl's gyrus, thalamus and estimated fornix disconnection, with nominal P values around 0.03–0.04 across a larger set of candidate regions and tracts. Tractotron estimates disconnection from a normative atlas rather than measuring each patient's tract directly. These are exploratory regional/disconnection associations, not an independently validated combined lesion map or proof of an episodic-memory mechanism. The abstract and Table 1 label 72 patients as having personal neglect, while the main Comb-test cutoff description gives 68/104 and the One Item test gives 31/86. These differing classifications are not silently reconciled; the catalogue reports the 104-person cohort and continuous-score analysis rather than inventing a single positive-group count.

For Lundblad, the clinical selection, normative and tactile-testing methods, imaging and lesion analyses, Results and principal Discussion and limitations were read. Patients were able to follow instructions and had no extinction, but hemispatial neglect was not independently tested. Lesion overlap in the 28 follow-up scans was too sparse for useful voxelwise inference. Only one very large sensorimotor network node met the study's criteria for exploratory testing; its one-sided comparison does not localize a specific gyrus. Predefined OP1, insular and dorsolateral frontal targets drew on healthy functional imaging and do not establish isolated causal sites in the heterogeneous lesions. No patient had a primary somatosensory cortical lesion, so background discussion of S1 does not justify painting S1. Normative age extrapolation, hand-score floor effects, selected clinical participation and incomplete follow-up limit generalization.

The remaining nine references retain abstract scope. Anatomical details absent from an abstract are not supplied from expectation or from a later review. In particular, Lewald's primary abstract reports three hemispherectomy patients, with impaired stationary localization; this takes precedence over an inconsistent later secondary summary. The catalogue now contains 61 full-text and 150 abstract-based reference records. Earlier addition reviews and the [full-text review log](full-text-review.md) retain their historical scopes.
